# Data Analysis Rules

How to present data so it actually drives decisions.

## Always

- Show trends, not just snapshots. A single number is rarely useful on its own.
- Include period-over-period comparisons: week over week, month over month, or cohort versus cohort depending on the metric.
- Use actual numbers, not just percentages.
- Flag anomalies and explain likely causes, including what would falsify the explanation.
- Separate observed data from inference.
- Include sample size, time window, and known data gaps when they affect confidence.

## Business Metrics

When working with acquisition, retention, monetization, or support data, tie movement back to the active priority stack:

- What changed?
- How much did it change in absolute and relative terms?
- Which user, customer, or revenue lever does it affect?
- What is the next action or decision this data supports?
